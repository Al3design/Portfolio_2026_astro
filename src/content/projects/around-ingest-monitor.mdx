          During my employment at Around MD, I worked on Ingest Monitor under a
          fixed-term contract, focusing on UX Research and UI Design. The

          The work concerns monitoring audiovisual material entering a media
          asset management system, or MAM. The interface brings signals and
          their states together so operators can observe what is happening and
          recognize situations that require attention.


          The design challenge lies in that combination: each signal needs to
          retain its identity while the overall view remains readable. The grid
          explorations, alerts and interaction adjustments below address
          different parts of the same problem.

      - _template: section
        title: Organizing signals while preserving readability
        text: >-
          The presentation compares arrangements of 24, 15 and 8 views. Each
          changes the relationship between the number of visible signals and the
          space available to read information about each one. These are
          composition alternatives documented in the work.


          It also distinguishes enlarging one signal from changing the
          arrangement of the entire panel. This difference helps explain the
          interaction: inspecting an element and reorganizing the full panel are
          actions with different scopes.


          The slides consider view aspect ratios and adaptation to different
          monitor sizes. The CSS Grid examples support that exploration of a
          responsive composition.
      - _template: section
        title: Making operational states visible
        text: >-
          The interface needs to communicate more than each signal’s image. It
          also needs to show whether a unit is recording, waiting or under
          maintenance, and whether an alert needs attention. Presentation
          examples include signal loss and black frames.


          The proposal combines messages, indicators and visual states. Reading
          a situation therefore relies on explicit information alongside color.
          In a multiview panel, these elements contribute to the hierarchy used
          to distinguish what is happening.


          Documented changes include a recording indicator, source-unit
          identification and a shift from a PIN reference to a lock state.
          Selection, dragging and locking variants complete the exploration of
          how views are manipulated.
